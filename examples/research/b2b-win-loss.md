# Research Questions: B2B Win / Loss

## Goal
Understand what factors actually drove a B2B buying decision — so the team can distinguish between product gaps, positioning gaps, sales process gaps, and noise.

## Screener criteria
- The primary buyer or economic decision-maker (not a champion who didn't have final say)
- Decision finalized within the last 4 weeks
- Willing to be candid about what drove the decision (screen out respondents who say "it was just a price decision" without any elaboration — they may not want to share real feedback)
- For losses: confirm they chose a competitor (not "not now" — that's a different interview)

## Opening (5 min)

1. Can you give me a bit of context on your team and what problem you were trying to solve when you started evaluating [product category]?
2. How did this evaluation come about — what triggered you to start looking?
3. Who was involved in the evaluation on your side?

*Purpose: understand the buying context and the committee, not just the individual.*

## Core questions (20–30 min)

### The evaluation
4. Walk me through how your evaluation worked. What did you do to compare options?

   *Probe: Did you run a trial, a pilot, a demo, or something else? Who drove the evaluation process?*

5. What were the 2–3 criteria that mattered most to your team going into it?

   *Probe: Were those criteria defined before the evaluation, or did they emerge during it?*

6. Did your priorities shift at all during the evaluation? Was there something you thought would matter that turned out not to, or something that surprised you?

   *Probe: What caused the shift?*

### The decision moment (for wins)
7. When did you feel like [product] was the right choice?

   *Probe: Was it a specific demo moment, a trial outcome, a reference call, or something else?*

8. Was there anything that almost made you go with another option?

   *Probe: What resolved that concern?*

9. What made you confident enough to move forward given any remaining uncertainties?

### The decision moment (for losses)
7. When did you feel like [product] wasn't going to be the right choice?

   *Probe: Was there a specific moment, or a slow build?*

8. What did [competitor they chose] do better than us in your evaluation?

   *Probe: Was it a product capability, the sales experience, pricing, or something else?*

9. If [product] had done [X] differently — would the outcome have changed?

   *Probe: Is this something we could have fixed, or was the fit just not right?*

### The broader context
10. Were there internal factors that affected the decision — politics, existing vendor relationships, timing pressures?

    *Probe: If you'd had more time, would you have evaluated differently?*

11. Looking back, was there anything in the process — demos, trials, proposals — that we did particularly well or particularly badly?

    *Probe: Compared to how other vendors handled the same stage?*

### Post-decision
12. [For wins] How confident do you feel now that you made the right choice?

    *Probe: What would change that confidence one way or the other in the next 6 months?*

    [For losses] Would you consider [product] again in the future under different circumstances?

    *Probe: What would need to be different?*

## Closing (5 min)

13. If you were advising our product team — not our sales team — what's the one thing you'd tell them to change or build to be more competitive in your market?

14. Is there anything about the evaluation or the decision that you think we'd be surprised to hear?

## What to code for
- **Decision criteria:** What 2–3 factors actually drove the decision (vs. what the sales team thought they were)
- **Evaluation quality:** Did our process (demo, trial, references) represent the product well? Where did it misrepresent?
- **Competitive differentiation:** What did the winner do better? Is it a product gap or a positioning gap?
- **Internal politics:** Which decisions were influenced by factors the product team can't affect (existing relationships, budget timing)?
- **Leading signals:** For wins — what moment in the evaluation was predictive? That's the moment to engineer more of.

## Common mistake with this guide
Letting the sales rep conduct the interview. Sales reps have a stake in the outcome and will unconsciously ask questions that confirm their view of why the deal was won or lost. They'll also leave out the uncomfortable parts. Win/loss interviews must be conducted by someone without a personal stake in the account — product, research, or an outside firm. The buyer will also speak more candidly to someone who isn't trying to win back or thank them.
