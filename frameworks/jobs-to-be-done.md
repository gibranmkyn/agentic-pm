# Jobs To Be Done (JTBD)

A framework for understanding why people adopt products by focusing on the **progress they're trying to make** — the "job" — rather than demographics, pain points, or feature requests.

The core insight: **people hire products to do jobs.** Two customers with identical demographics can hire entirely different products because they have different jobs. A Snickers gets hired for a quick hunger fix; a Milky Way gets hired for a comfort treat — same category, different jobs.

**Origins & authors:**
- **Clay Christensen** — *Competing Against Luck* (2016). The strategic, philosophical framing.
- **Bob Moesta** — *Demand-Side Sales* (2020). The narrative, interview-driven practitioner method.
- **Tony Ulwick** — *Jobs to Be Done* (2016). The quantitative Outcome-Driven Innovation (ODI) method.

---

## Core principles

- **Context and outcome, not pain and gain.** The biggest JTBD misconception is treating it as a pain-finding exercise. The real question is: *in what context* did someone decide "today's the day I do something different"?
- **People hire products.** The language is deliberate — hiring implies a job, a context for the decision, and the possibility of being fired.
- **Struggling moments create demand.** Demand doesn't come from products — it comes from situations where someone is stuck enough to act.
- **Revealed behavior beats stated preference.** In Moesta's research, 93% of people who said they wanted an Energy Star house did not buy one for that reason.

---

## The Four Forces (what governs the switch moment)

| Force | Pushing toward switch | Pulling away |
|---|---|---|
| **F1: Push of current situation** | Dissatisfaction with what exists | |
| **F2: Pull toward new outcome** | Attraction of the better future | |
| **F3: Anxiety of the new solution** | | Fear of the unknown |
| **F4: Habit of the present** | | Attachment to the current way |

**Rule:** If F1 + F2 > F3 + F4, the person switches. Otherwise, they stay.

**Implication most teams miss:** *reducing anxiety (F3) often beats adding features (F2).* A cheaper price, a guarantee, or a "no credit card required" trial can move more people than a new capability.

---

## Interview methodology (Moesta's approach)

- **Only interview people who've already switched.** People who "plan to" can't commit their future behavior. Talk to recent hirers and firers.
- **No discussion guides.** Follow the causal thread of the switch story, not a pre-written script.
- **Two rounds of 12 > one round of 24.** Do 12, find patterns, do 12 more with those patterns as hypotheses.
- **Three layers of language.** Surface (what they say) → Fantasy/nightmare (exaggerated) → Causal reality (what actually drove them). Layer 3 is the goal.

---

## Six phases of the buying process

1. Passive looking (problem unaware)
2. Active looking (solution seeking)
3. Deciding (evaluating options)
4. First use
5. Ongoing use / habit formation
6. Churn / switch to something else

Autobooks 4x'd conversion by identifying which phase their prospects were in and adjusting messaging to match.

## Which JTBD variant to use

| Variant | Approach | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Moesta's** | Narrative, interview-driven, causal switch story | Product discovery; understanding demand. Pairs well with Teresa Torres's Continuous Discovery Habits. |
| **Ulwick's ODI** | Functional, quantitative; importance × satisfaction scoring | Systematic prioritization across a known outcome set; risk-mature orgs with research budget. |
| **Christensen's** | Strategic, philosophical | Company-level innovation decisions; investor/exec conversations. |

**Don't mix vocabularies mid-project.** Moesta's "struggling moments" and Ulwick's "unmet outcomes" are adjacent concepts, not interchangeable. Pick one variant per initiative.

---

## When NOT to use JTBD

- Habitual, low-engagement purchases (buying gum, routine re-stocking) — no memorable switch moment to excavate.
- Compliance purchases where the buyer has no real choice.
- Pre-launch products with no hirers/firers yet — run demand-testing interviews instead.

---

## Application in product strategy

Feed JTBD findings into your [Opportunity Solution Tree](opportunity-solution-tree.md): the "struggling moments" you surface become opportunities. Then use the Four Forces to evaluate solutions — a solution that reduces F3 (anxiety) may outperform one that increases F2 (pull).
