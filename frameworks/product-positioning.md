# Product Positioning

Positioning is the context customers use to interpret a product's value. It's not the product. It's the frame inside which customers understand what the product does, who it's for, and why it beats the alternatives. Weak positioning is the root cause of most "why doesn't the market get it?" problems — not a messaging problem, not a product problem.

When salespeople consistently reframe the product mid-pitch, positioning is wrong. That's the tell.

**Author:** April Dunford — *Obviously Awesome* (Page Two, 2019). See [aprildunford.com](https://aprildunford.com).

---

## Five-step methodology (sequential)

### Step 1 — Competitive alternatives
Start with one question: if your product didn't exist, what would customers use instead? The real competitor is often not another SaaS tool — it could be Excel, email, a manual process, or simply doing nothing. This sets the baseline for all differentiation claims that follow.

### Step 2 — Differentiated capabilities
List the features or capabilities your product has that the competitive alternative from Step 1 lacks. These are capabilities that only matter *relative to the specific alternative* — a different alternative produces a different capability list.

### Step 3 — Value translation
For each differentiated capability, ask: "So what?" What does this capability enable the customer to do that they couldn't do before? Keep asking until you reach a business outcome the customer already cares about — time saved, revenue gained, risk reduced. This converts feature descriptions into value claims.

### Step 4 — Best-fit customer profile
Given the value claims from Step 3, ask: who cares most about these things? Which customers have the problem you solve most acutely, are positioned to act on it soonest, and will achieve the best outcomes? This is not your total addressable market — it's your beachhead, the customers where your positioning is most compelling.

### Step 5 — Market category
Choose the label that sets customer expectations in the most favorable way for your differentiated value. The category creates the purchase frame. A wrong category creates the wrong competitive set and the wrong evaluation criteria.

Many companies inadvertently pick categories where they will always lose. A product that beats Excel on automation but loses to Salesforce on CRM depth should not call itself a CRM.

## Key principles

- **Positioning is a choice, not a discovery.** You construct it deliberately. You don't find it through research.
- **Context creates value.** The same product seems cheap or expensive, powerful or limited, depending on the comparison set its category establishes.
- **The sales team is your positioning barometer.** When sales consistently rewrites the pitch, the official positioning is wrong.
- **Positioning precedes messaging.** Messaging is execution; positioning is strategy.

---

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Defaulting to an established category out of ease | Ask: do you win on the standard criteria of that category? If not, don't be in that category. |
| Feature-list marketing | Apply Step 3 (value translation) — connect capabilities to outcomes the customer already cares about |
| "It's like X, but for Y" | This positions you as inferior to X rather than best-in-class for Y |
| Positioning written by marketing without sales input | Salespeople know what works in real pitches; include them in the process |

## When NOT to use it

- For internal product strategy when there's no external audience — use [Strategy Kernel](strategy-kernel.md) or [Six Dimensions](six-dimensions-of-product-strategy.md) for internal work.
- Before product-market fit — positioning a product whose value proposition is still being validated creates misleading external signal that's hard to undo.
