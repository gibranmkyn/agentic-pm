# Six Dimensions of Product Strategy

Most startup failures are not random. They cluster around skipped dimensions: a team builds a great product for a well-defined problem but has no theory for how to grow, or assumes a business model that never gets validated. Sachin Rekhi's six-dimensions framework makes all strategic assumptions explicit — so you can see what's missing and prioritize what to test.

The framework is not a template to fill in once. It produces a working hypothesis document that gets updated as you learn. The six dimensions are also the direct input to Riskiest Hypothesis Sequencing: whichever dimension carries the least conviction is where validation should focus first.

**Author:** Sachin Rekhi — *Deliberate Startup* (Reforge, 2022). See [sachinrekhi.com](https://sachinrekhi.com).

---

## The six dimensions

### 1. Problem to solve
What specific pain are you addressing, and how acute is it? The painkiller-vs.-vitamin test applies here: a vitamin is nice to have; a painkiller is something people actively seek. If it's not clear which you have, the rest of the strategy sits on uncertain ground.

### 2. Target customer
Who specifically is your "bullseye" customer? Not a broad segment — a nameable, describable profile with specific demographics, firmographics, or psychographics. Lean methodologies often encourage building for the broadest possible audience early; this framework reverses that default, because building for everyone typically means serving no one well enough to create genuine word-of-mouth or retention.

### 3. Value proposition
What specific value do you create for the target customer? How do you solve the problem better than what they'd use without you?

### 4. Strategic differentiation
Why will you win against alternatives? What moat, insight, or unique capability prevents competitors from copying you once the value proposition is validated?

### 5. Growth strategy
How will you acquire the exact target audience you're going after? This is not a marketing plan — it's a hypothesis about which acquisition mechanism (sales-led, product-led, community-led, content, paid) works for your specific customer at your specific stage, and what a scalable growth loop looks like long-term.

### 6. Business model
How will you make money? Critically: does the monetization mechanism align with the value you create? A free-forever model is hard to test willingness-to-pay; a usage-based model has different unit economics than seat-based. The business model hypothesis is often the last one teams write — and therefore the most frequently the riskiest one.

## How to apply it

1. **Write a PMF narrative document** — one to two pages covering all six dimensions as explicit hypotheses, not conclusions. The goal is to make assumptions visible, not to make them look confident.
2. **Check for missing dimensions.** The most common omissions are Growth Strategy (teams assume good product = word-of-mouth) and Business Model (teams defer the monetization question until "later").
3. **Identify the riskiest dimension.** For each dimension, ask: how much conviction do we actually have? Which one, if wrong, would kill the product? That's where to focus validation first. See [Riskiest Hypothesis Sequencing](riskiest-hypothesis-sequencing.md).
4. **Update after each validation cycle.** The six dimensions are working hypotheses, not a one-time plan. After every significant customer learning, check whether any dimension needs updating.

---

## Common failure modes

| Failure | What happens |
|---|---|
| Skipping Growth Strategy | The product works but can't scale; "we'll figure out distribution later" becomes "we never did" |
| Vague target customer | Customer interviews produce conflicting signals; the team can't build for anyone specifically |
| Untested Business Model | The product gets validated but the revenue model doesn't; pivoting late is expensive |
| Treating the document as final | The PMF narrative is a living hypothesis, not a strategic commitment; teams that stop updating it after launch stop learning |

## When NOT to use it

- For a mature product with confirmed PMF and a known competitive set — you need [Strategy Kernel](strategy-kernel.md) or [Strategic Narrative](strategic-narrative.md), not a hypothesis document.
- When you already have strong conviction on all six dimensions — at that point, the framework has served its purpose; move to execution.
