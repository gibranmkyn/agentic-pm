# Research Questions: First-Run / New User Discovery

## Goal
Observe what a new user actually does in their first session — what draws their attention, where they get confused, and whether they can articulate what the product does and why they'd return.

## Screener criteria
- Never used [product] before (confirm explicitly)
- Fits the target user profile (define specifically: role, context, relevant experience level)
- Comfortable thinking out loud during a task (ask in screener: "Would you be comfortable narrating what you're thinking as you use something for the first time?")
- Exclude researchers, designers, and product people — they observe products differently from typical users

## Opening (5 min)

*Before starting the session:*

1. Can you tell me a bit about your work and how you typically [task relevant to product category]?
2. Before I share anything about [product] — what are you currently using for [relevant task]? How's that going?
3. In a moment I'm going to give you access to [product] and ask you to just use it as you normally would. I'll ask you to think out loud as you go — just say what you're noticing and what you're doing.

*Open the product. Do not introduce it or explain what it does. Observe.*

## Core questions (during live observation — probes only, 25 min)

*These are prompts to use during observation, not a scripted sequence:*

4. What are you looking for right now?
5. What do you think that does?
6. What were you expecting to happen?
7. What made you decide to click that?
8. Where did you think this would go?

*Do not help. Do not answer questions about the product. If they get stuck, note the stuck point and let them sit in it for 60 seconds before asking: "What would you do next if I wasn't here?"*

## Debrief questions (20 min)

### Comprehension
9. Now that you've had a look around — what do you think [product] is for?

   *Probe: Who do you think uses this?*

10. Was there anything that surprised you — something you didn't expect to find, or something you expected that wasn't there?

### Value
11. Is there a task you do regularly that this might help with?

    *Probe: Walk me through the last time you did that task. Would [product] have changed anything about how you did it?*

12. Did anything in the first session make you think "I could actually use this"?

    *Probe: What was it? Was it what you came looking for, or a surprise?*

### Friction
13. Was there a moment where you weren't sure what to do next, or where something didn't work the way you expected?

    *Probe: What did you try? What would have helped?*

14. On a scale from "I'd come back tomorrow" to "I'd never think about this again" — where do you land right now, and why?

### Memory
15. A week from now, what do you think you'd remember about what you saw today?

    *Probe: What made that stick?*

## Closing (5 min)

16. If you were explaining [product] to a colleague who had the same problem you do — what would you say it does?

17. What's the one thing that would need to be different for you to actually start using it?

## What to code for
- **First action:** What's the first thing they click or try? Does it match what you expected the entry point to be?
- **Stuck moments:** Where do they stop, backtrack, or look confused? Note the specific element (label, layout, concept)
- **Value comprehension:** Can they articulate what the product does and for whom? If they can't after 25 minutes, the positioning is broken
- **Surprise elements:** What did they find that they didn't expect? Both positive and negative surprises are signal
- **Intent match:** Does the task they would use this for match what you built it for?

## Common mistake with this guide
Introducing the product before starting the observation. "This is a tool that helps you do X" biases everything that follows — they'll look for X rather than discovering what they would naturally look for. The most valuable data in a first-run session comes from the first 3 minutes when they have no framing. Protect that window.
