# Research Questions: Opportunity Discovery

## Goal
Surface unmet needs grounded in specific behavioral stories — so the opportunity tree gets populated with customer experiences, not brainstormed assumptions.

## Screener criteria
- Actively working in the domain being explored (not aspirational users — people doing the job today)
- Deals with the outcome area at least weekly (not occasionally)
- Can recall specific instances from the last 30 days, not just general impressions
- Exclude people in purely advisory roles (consultants, advisors) — their experience of the problem is second-hand

## Opening (5 min)

1. Can you tell me about your role and what your day-to-day looks like in relation to [domain area]?
2. How long have you been doing this kind of work?
3. What does a typical week look like — walk me through last week briefly.

*Purpose: get a concrete timeline grounded in their actual life, not a job description recitation.*

## Core questions (40–45 min)

### Current workflow
4. Walk me through the last time you had to [task in the domain area]. What happened?

   *Probe: Take me step by step. What did you do first?*

5. What tools, apps, or approaches did you use for each step?

   *Probe: Were those the things you wished you had, or the things you had available?*

6. Where in that process did you slow down, stop, or have to work around something?

   *Probe: How long have you been dealing with that friction? Have you tried to fix it?*

### Pain and workarounds
7. Is there a part of [domain area] that takes significantly more time or effort than you think it should?

   *Probe: Walk me through the last time that happened. What were you trying to accomplish, and what got in the way?*

8. Are there things you've built yourself — spreadsheets, templates, processes, checklists — to compensate for something that doesn't exist or doesn't work well enough?

   *Probe: When did you build that? What made you decide to build it rather than find a tool?*

9. Is there a task in this area you dread or try to avoid?

   *Probe: Why? What makes it hard or unpleasant?*

### Outcomes and stakes
10. What happens when this area goes well versus when it goes wrong?

    *Probe: Can you give me an example of each — a recent time it worked and a recent time it didn't?*

11. Who else is affected when things go wrong here — just you, or other people too?

    *Probe: How does that play out? What do they see or experience?*

### Existing solutions
12. Have you tried tools or approaches specifically designed for this? What happened?

    *Probe: What made you keep using it / stop using it?*

13. Is there anything in [adjacent domain] that handles a similar problem well that you wish existed here?

    *Probe: What specifically does it do that would translate?*

### What good looks like
14. Imagine this area worked perfectly for you — not a specific tool, just the outcome. What would be different about your day or week?

    *Probe: What would you be able to do that you can't now? Or what would just disappear from your life?*

15. If you could wave a wand and fix one thing in this area — just one — what would it be?

    *Probe: Why that one over everything else?*

## Closing (5 min)

16. Is there anything about how you work in this area that you think is different from how most people in your situation approach it?

17. Is there anything I should have asked about that I didn't?

## What to code for
- **Specific stuck moments:** Named steps in a workflow where they slow down, stop, or build workarounds — these are opportunity candidates
- **Workarounds and DIY solutions:** Things they've built themselves to compensate — these signal both pain intensity and under-served needs
- **Stakes:** What happens when it goes wrong? High stakes = high willingness to pay / change behavior for a good solution
- **Existing solutions tried:** What have they already attempted? Why didn't it work? This tells you what "close but not right" looks like
- **Ideal outcome:** Not a feature request — a description of what life would look like without the friction. The best opportunity framing comes from here

## Common mistake with this guide
Asking about the domain in the abstract and getting a general description of problems. "What's hard about X?" produces opinion-level answers. "Walk me through the last time you had to do X" forces behavioral recall and produces specific, quotable, placeable stories. Every question in this guide should be anchored to a recent specific event if possible. If a participant keeps speaking generally, re-anchor: "Can you give me a specific recent example of that?"
