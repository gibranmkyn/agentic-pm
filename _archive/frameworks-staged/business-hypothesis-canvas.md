# Business Hypothesis Canvas

Most early-stage teams hold their strategy in their heads. No written artifact, no explicit assumptions, no way to tell if you were right or wrong when things change. The Business Hypothesis Canvas solves this: a one-page document that makes strategic bets explicit, forces triage, and serves as a shared coordination artifact for the whole team.

The canvas is not a plan. It's a container for liquid strategy — structure without false precision.

**Author:** Brian Balfour — *Business Hypothesis Canvas* (Reforge, 2023). See [brianbalfour.com](https://brianbalfour.com) and Reforge.

---

## Eight dimensions

For each dimension, the canvas asks for three buckets: **focus now** / **relevant for later** / **not in scope**. The "not in scope" bucket is as important as the "focus now" bucket — explicit exclusions are strategy too.

| Dimension | What to hypothesize |
|---|---|
| **Target audience** | Who is the beachhead customer? Define narrowly enough that you could name 10 of them. Define what you are NOT serving. |
| **Problem** | What specific underserved pain does this audience have? Is it a painkiller or a vitamin? |
| **Value proposition** | What specific value do you deliver, and why is it meaningfully better than what exists? |
| **Distribution / acquisition** | How will people find and start using the product? What's the first scalable channel hypothesis? |
| **Monetization** | How and how much will you charge? What's the model and the mechanism? |
| **Defensibility** | What will make this hard to copy over time? (Hold this loosely early — the real moat usually becomes clear only through operating.) |
| **Future expansion** | What are the next-ring opportunities after the beachhead? Not today's focus, but the path. |
| **Alternatives** | What do customers compare you to psychologically — not just direct competitors but the full substitution space? |

---

## How to apply it

1. **Write the first draft alone in 30 minutes.** Speed over polish — the goal is to make your current assumptions visible, not impressive.
2. **Review with your team.** Where do people disagree? Disagreements reveal which hypotheses aren't actually shared — these are the riskiest.
3. **Apply the triage.** For each dimension: is this now, later, or not at all? The "not at all" bucket is where teams usually struggle. Forcing it builds focus.
4. **Check internal consistency.** High pricing → paid acquisition is likely not viable. Narrow targeting → viral growth will be slow. Walk through the dependencies.
5. **Set a review cadence.** For slow-feedback products (enterprise, education): every 6 months. For fast-feedback products (consumer): monthly.

---

## The most important and most often skipped dimension

**Not-in-scope exclusions.** Most teams write what they're doing. Few write what they're explicitly not doing. But exclusions are what create focus. If every feature request, market segment, and distribution channel is "maybe future," you have no focus.

The Reforge example: explicitly not serving career switchers and entry-level professionals in 2016 was as important a strategic choice as serving experienced practitioners.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Strategy lives only in the founder's head | Write the canvas; any disagreement surfaces on the canvas, not in execution |
| Updating after a single data point | Wait for a pattern of 3+ signals before updating a hypothesis |
| Skipping the "not at all" bucket | Force it — pick 2–3 explicit exclusions per relevant dimension |
| Treating the defensibility hypothesis as certain | It will likely be wrong; that's expected. Discover the real moat through operating. |

## When NOT to use it

- Post-PMF with a confirmed business model — at that stage, a [Strategy Kernel](strategy-kernel.md) or [Six Dimensions](six-dimensions-of-product-strategy.md) review is more appropriate than a hypothesis canvas.
- When the team is in pure execution mode — the canvas is a thinking tool, not an execution tracker.
