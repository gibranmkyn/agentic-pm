# Research Questions: Pricing Sensitivity

## Goal
Understand how users perceive the value of your product relative to price — and identify the price points where they'd feel the deal was clearly worth it, clearly too expensive, and clearly too cheap to trust.

## Screener criteria
- The economic decision-maker: either pays for the product themselves or approves the purchase for their team
- Active user or currently evaluating (not churned — their price sensitivity has already been expressed)
- Familiar enough with the product to have a value opinion (at least 30 days of real use, or a full trial)
- Exclude early adopters who got a legacy deal and haven't thought about price in years — they're not representative

## Opening (5 min)

1. Can you give me a quick sense of how you or your team uses [product] — what's the main thing it helps you do?
2. How did you end up choosing [product] over other options?
3. When you think about the value you get from [product], what comes to mind first?

*Purpose: anchor on the value they experience before you introduce any price framing.*

## Core questions (30–35 min)

### Value anchoring
4. What's the biggest thing [product] helps you accomplish that you couldn't do as easily without it?

   *Probe: Can you put a rough number on what that's worth to you — in time saved, revenue generated, or cost avoided?*

5. Is there a task or outcome [product] enables that you'd pay specifically for — something you'd happily pay for even if everything else disappeared?

   *Probe: Why that one? What makes it worth paying for separately?*

6. What would you lose if you had to stop using [product] tomorrow? How long would it take you to replace that?

   *Probe: What would you use instead? How much would that cost?*

### Price sensitivity (Van Westendorp framing — ask these in order)
7. At what monthly price would [product] start to feel like it's getting expensive — not prohibitively expensive, but you'd start wondering if it's worth it?

   *Probe: What would need to change about the product at that price to make it feel right?*

8. At what monthly price would [product] feel so expensive that you'd seriously consider stopping, even if you still liked it?

   *Probe: At that price, what would you do instead?*

9. At what monthly price would [product] feel so cheap that you'd wonder if it's still being properly maintained or if something's wrong?

   *Probe: What gives you that feeling — is it about the quality of the product, or comparison to alternatives?*

10. At what monthly price would [product] feel like a great deal — clearly worth it?

    *Probe: What makes that price feel right?*

### Packaging and structure
11. If you could choose between paying a flat monthly fee and paying based on how much you use it, which would you prefer?

    *Probe: Why? Does your usage vary enough that one model would clearly cost you more or less?*

12. Are there features or parts of [product] you don't use that you feel like you're paying for anyway?

    *Probe: If those were separated out, would you pay less for a stripped-down version, or would you pay about the same?*

### Comparison
13. What else do you spend money on for the same goal [product] serves? How does [product]'s price compare?

    *Probe: Do you feel like you're getting more or less value per dollar from [product] vs. those alternatives?*

14. If [product]'s price went up 20%, what would you do?

    *Probe: Would you definitely stay, probably stay, or start evaluating alternatives?*

## Closing (5 min)

15. Is the current pricing of [product] something you'd describe as fair, a good deal, or too expensive — and why?

16. Is there anything about how [product] is priced that confuses you or that you think could be clearer?

## What to code for
- **Value anchors:** The specific outcomes users would pay for even if everything else disappeared — these anchor the price ceiling
- **Van Westendorp thresholds:** The four price points named (too cheap / acceptable / expensive / too expensive) — plot them to find the acceptable price range
- **Comparison benchmarks:** What else do they pay for the same job? This tells you whether you're being benchmarked against expensive tools or cheap ones
- **Feature overhang:** Parts of the product they feel like they're paying for but don't use — signals packaging opportunity
- **Churn threshold:** At what % increase do they say they'd evaluate alternatives? That's your pricing ceiling for the current product state

## Common mistake with this guide
Asking "what would you pay for this?" without first anchoring on value. Price perception is relative — users will name a number based on their last reference point (a competitor's price, a SaaS category norm) not on the actual value you deliver. Anchor on value first (questions 4–6), then introduce price framing. Users who have articulated "this saves me 5 hours a week" will name a higher WTP than users who haven't.
