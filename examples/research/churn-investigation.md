# Research Questions: Churn Investigation

## Goal
Identify the specific trigger events and unmet needs that caused users to leave — so the team can distinguish between fixable product problems and unavoidable churn.

## Screener criteria
- Cancelled their account or went inactive in the last 30 days
- Were active for at least 60 days before churning (exclude trial churn — different problem)
- Willing to be candid: screen out participants who seem primarily motivated by getting the product back for free

## Opening (5 min)

1. Before we get into your experience with [product], can you tell me a bit about what you were using it for?
2. How long were you using it before you decided to stop?
3. When was the last time you used it before cancelling — what were you doing?

*Purpose: re-anchor them in the actual product experience, not the moment of cancellation.*

## Core questions (20–30 min)

### The decay
4. Looking back, when did you first start feeling like [product] wasn't quite doing what you needed?

   *Probe: Was it a gradual thing, or did something specific happen?*

5. What were you trying to do around that time?

   *Probe: Was this something you'd done successfully before, or was this a new need?*

6. What did you do when you hit that point — did you look for a workaround, try to figure it out, or just live with it?

   *Probe: How long did you try before deciding to cancel?*

### The trigger
7. Walk me through the last week or two before you decided to cancel. What happened?

   *Probe: Was there a specific moment that made you decide, or did you just stop caring?*

8. Before you cancelled, was there anything [product] could have done that would have made you stay?

   *Probe: Was it a missing feature, a price issue, a support problem, or something else?*

### The alternative
9. After you cancelled, what did you do instead? Did you find something else, or did you just stop doing the thing entirely?

   *Probe: How is that working out? Is it actually solving the problem?*

10. What does the alternative do that [product] didn't — if anything?

    *Probe: Is that something [product] could have offered, or is it fundamentally different?*

### Looking back
11. Is there anything about [product] that you actually miss or that you think was genuinely good?

    *Probe: If that thing got better, would you reconsider?*

12. If someone in your situation asked you whether to try [product], what would you tell them?

    *Probe: What's the one thing you'd warn them about?*

## Closing (5 min)

13. Is there anything about your experience that you think we really need to understand — something you're surprised we haven't fixed?

14. If you were rebuilding [product] from scratch for someone in your situation, what's the one thing you'd make sure it got right?

## What to code for
- **Trigger event:** The specific moment or incident that initiated the cancellation decision (not the reason given in the exit survey)
- **Duration of decay:** How long was the user dissatisfied before cancelling? Short = product problem; long = habituation then sudden trigger
- **Fixability:** Is the reason for churn something the product could have addressed, or a life/context change (e.g., job change, budget cut, situation resolved)?
- **Alternative behavior:** What did they switch to? If they switched to nothing, the pain wasn't high enough — different problem than switching to a competitor
- **Bright spots:** What was actually working? This helps separate the fixable from the permanent

## Common mistake with this guide
Running exit surveys instead of interviews and treating the survey responses as the real reason. Exit surveys capture the rational explanation users are comfortable sharing publicly. The switch story interview surfaces the actual emotional trigger and the specific moment — which is almost always different from the survey answer. "Price was too high" in a survey often means "I stopped feeling like I was getting my money's worth" in the interview — which is a product problem, not a pricing problem.
