# Foundation Sprint

A two-day (~10 hour) structured process for pre-seed founders to align on the fundamental building blocks of their product before writing code. Fills the gap the Design Sprint assumes you've already solved: who your customer is, what problem you're solving, and why you're different.

**Authors:** Jake Knapp + John Zeratsky — *Click* (2025); Character Capital Foundation Sprint methodology (2022). See also Knapp's earlier work: *Sprint* (2016).

---

## The problem it solves

The Design Sprint (five days: map, sketch, decide, prototype, test) assumes you already know your customer, your problem, and your differentiation. Most pre-seed founders haven't rigorously aligned on these answers — not because they haven't thought about them, but because they've never forced the whole team to write individual answers in silence and then compare.

The Foundation Sprint is the prerequisite to the Design Sprint. Character Capital runs it with every new investment as the first thing after writing a check.

---

## Structure

### Phase 1 — The Basics (Day 1, first half)

Each person writes individual answers to five questions, working in silence:
1. Who is the most important customer?
2. What problem are we solving for that customer?
3. What is the competition (broadly: any existing solution the customer uses)?
4. What is our key capability, insight, or advantage?
5. What is our motivation as a team?

The decision-maker picks one answer for each after hearing all perspectives. Result: a "basics sheet" that everyone can see and challenge.

### Phase 2 — Differentiation (Day 1, second half)

Map the product on a 2x2 against competitors using a chosen differentiator axis. Classic differentiators: fast/slow, smart/simple, easy/hard, networked/siloed. Custom differentiators (specific to your category) are more compelling when authentic.

The "Loserville" rule: the bottom-left, top-left, and bottom-right quadrants form an L — don't be there. You must be in the top-right.

Differentiation passes two tests:
1. Can you actually deliver on it?
2. Do customers care about and believe it?

### Phase 3 — Approach and founding hypothesis (Day 1, end)

"Magic lenses" activity: each person independently writes answers to "What would [famous company/role model] do with this problem?" Breaks teams out of category assumptions.

The **founding hypothesis** — a single sentence: *"For [customer], who has [problem], [product name] is a [category] that [differentiator], unlike [competitor] which [weakness]."*

Every element is a testable prediction. This is the output that Day 2 validates.

### Day 2 — Prototype and Test

Build a rough prototype (low-fi is fine). Show it to 5 real customers. Goal: yes/no signal on the founding hypothesis, not polish. AI prototyping tools can speed this up, though care is needed not to let execution quality bias the feedback signal.

## Silent parallel work — the key mechanism

The sprint's effectiveness relies on note-before-discuss: everyone writes their individual answer first, then all answers are revealed simultaneously. This prevents anchoring and surfaces hidden disagreements that conversations never would.

> "The moment where you immediately start to see value is when everyone on the team writes down their own answer to the same question and you see how different they all are." — Jake Knapp

---

## The founding hypothesis as testable prediction

The output is a testable sentence: *"If we help [customer] solve [problem] with [approach], they will choose it over [competitors] because our solution is [differentiators]."*

Examples from Knapp:
- Gmail: solve inbox overflow better than Outlook/Hotmail/Yahoo because more storage and great search
- Slack: solve workplace communication better than email because channels + search + integrations

Each element is a prediction. After the Foundation Sprint, Design Sprints validate each element.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Discussion before writing | Enforce silent writing first — anchoring kills divergence |
| Picking a differentiator customers don't care about | Test both: can you deliver it, and do customers value it? |
| Skipping Day 2 (prototype + test) | A hypothesis without a test is a strategy workshop, not a sprint |
| Using the sprint for a mature product | Foundation Sprint is for pre-seed alignment; use Design Sprint for feature-level exploration |

---

## When NOT to use it

- You already have product-market fit. Use Design Sprints for feature exploration instead.
- You're a solo founder with no team disagreements to surface. The sprint's value is in surfacing hidden misalignment.
- You need deep market research first. The sprint assumes basic domain knowledge exists.
