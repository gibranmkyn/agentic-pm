# SPIDER Decision Framework

Decisions made in meetings get relitigated. Meetings have no memory — nobody agrees on what was decided, who decided it, or why the alternatives were rejected. SPIDER is the artifact that replaces the meeting as the primary decision medium.

SPIDER stands for: **Situation → People → Ideas → Decision → Explanation → Rollout.**

Developed by Tatyana Mamut (Pendo, Nextdoor, Salesforce, Amazon) for high-ambiguity product innovation work — environments where you can't AB test your way to clarity and where organizational opinion is abundant.

**Author:** Tatyana Mamut — *Hard Decisions in Product Innovation* (Reforge, 2024). See Reforge course materials.

---

## The six components

### S — Situation
What problem are we solving, and what makes this decision necessary? Write this so someone unfamiliar with the project can understand why the decision matters. This section forces the advocate to articulate the context before anyone has anchoring from options.

### P — People
Four roles must be explicitly named. None can be blank.

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| **Advocate** | Owns the SPIDER document; drives the decision and makes the recommendation |
| **Approver** | Final decision authority — the buck stops here; cannot be skipped |
| **Input-givers** | People who must be consulted before the decision is finalized; if anyone is missing, pause and consult before sending |
| **Aligned** | Informed after the decision is made; need to understand and support the rollout |

If you notice an important function wasn't consulted (marketing, legal, a key partner team), note it and consult them before finalizing.

### I — Ideas
List every idea being considered — including ones the advocate considers infeasible or bad. This is the most psychologically important section.

**The key insight**: people care more about being heard than about winning. If an approver sees their idea acknowledged in writing with a clear explanation of why it wasn't selected, they almost never relitigate. If their idea is absent from the document, they relitigate — even if you told them privately.

Present each idea with neutral pros and cons. Do not advocate for any option in this section.

### D — Decision
In standard situations: the advocate fills in their recommendation before sending to the approver. In high-stakes political situations: leave this blank and let the approver fill it in without being anchored by the advocate's preference.

**Always include the decision date.** The date is the most commonly omitted field — and "when was this decided?" is the most common reopener.

### E — Explanation
Why this idea was selected over the others. Brief and objective. This is what gets quoted a year later when someone asks why the decision was made.

### R — Rollout
Implementation owner and next steps per function (engineering, sales, marketing, operations), with timelines. Rollout clarity prevents the second most common decision failure: "we decided, but nobody did anything."

## How to apply it

1. **Calibrate depth to stakes.** One-way door decisions (irreversible) and significant two-way door decisions deserve the full SPIDER document. Team-level or day-to-day decisions get a mini-SPIDER: "We're deciding between X and Y; we chose Z because [one sentence]."
2. **Start with Situation and Ideas before the meeting.** Write the context and list all options before the group convenes. This frames the decision and gets your input heard without requiring attendance.
3. **Post it in a shareable, linkable location.** The document is only useful if it can be referenced. A Google Doc in a known folder, a Notion page, a Confluence link — the mechanism matters less than the URL being findable.
4. **Use it retroactively.** If a contentious decision was made without a SPIDER, write one retroactively and share it. This introduces the practice as a service, not a critique.

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| No approver named | "Everyone decides" means no one does. One person must own the final call. |
| Ideas section omits the losing options | People whose ideas aren't listed relitigate. List everything neutrally. |
| No date on the decision | The most overlooked field. Add it. |
| Input-givers consulted after the decision | Consult them *before* the Decision field is filled in. |
| Document lives only in email | Email isn't searchable by the team six months later. Post it somewhere discoverable. |

---

## When NOT to use it

- Routine operational decisions that truly don't matter in 6 months — apply the mini-SPIDER or make the call and move on.
- Decisions made under time pressure where the SPIDER process would itself cause harm — note the decision informally and write it up afterward.
