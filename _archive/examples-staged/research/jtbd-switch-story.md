# Research Questions: JTBD Switch Story

## Goal
Understand what caused someone to stop tolerating their old solution and hire yours instead (or fire yours and hire something else) — so you can identify the pushes, pulls, anxieties, and habits that govern switching.

## Screener criteria
- Switched to or from your product within the last 60 days (not earlier — memory degrades fast)
- Made the switch themselves or were the primary decision-maker (not installed by IT without input)
- Exclude participants currently in a trial or evaluation period — they haven't committed yet

## Opening (5 min)

1. Tell me a bit about your work and what you use [product category] for day to day.
2. Before we get into the specifics of your switch, can you walk me back to roughly when you started thinking about making a change?
3. What was life like before the switch — what tool or approach were you using?

*Purpose: set the timeline, not collect data. You're helping them re-enter the mental state of that period.*

## Core questions (25–35 min)

### The first thought
4. What was the first moment you remember thinking "this isn't working anymore"? What was happening that day?

   *Probe: Was it a specific event, or a slow build? If slow build — can you name the last straw?*

5. What were you trying to accomplish when that thought hit?

   *Probe: Was this a task you'd done before without a problem, or was this the first time you'd needed to do it?*

### The search
6. When you decided to look for an alternative, what did you do first?

   *Probe: Who did you ask? What did you search? Did you know what you were looking for, or were you exploring?*

7. What options did you seriously consider before choosing?

   *Probe: What made you narrow it down to those options specifically?*

8. Was there a moment where you almost didn't switch — something that made you hesitate?

   *Probe: What was the hesitation about — cost, risk of being wrong, learning curve, something else?*

### The decision moment
9. Walk me through the day you made the final decision to switch. What happened?

   *Probe: Was there something specific that pushed you over the line, or did you just run out of patience?*

10. What were you most worried about getting wrong with the switch?

    *Probe: Did any of those worries turn out to be real?*

### The outcome
11. Now that you've been using [new product / alternative] for a few weeks — what do you actually like about it that you didn't expect?

    *Probe: Was this something you were looking for, or a surprise?*

12. What do you miss about what you left behind?

    *Probe: Is there anything you've had to give up or work around?*

13. If a colleague in the same situation as you were asked me next week, what would you tell them to do?

## Closing (5 min)

14. Is there anything about your experience — before, during, or after the switch — that you think I should know but haven't asked about?

15. If you could change one thing about how the product you chose handles this, what would it be?

*Thank them. Ask if they're open to follow-up in 3–4 weeks.*

## What to code for
- **Push forces:** What made the old solution intolerable (the specific moment, not the general frustration)
- **Pull forces:** What specifically attracted them to the new solution (concrete, not "it was better")
- **Anxieties:** What almost stopped the switch (price, risk, habits, social proof)
- **Habits:** What are they still doing the old way out of muscle memory, even after switching?
- **First thought:** The triggering event — this is the job that needs to be done, named precisely

## Common mistake with this guide
Asking "why did you choose us?" instead of reconstructing the switch chronologically. That question invites rationalization — people will give you a clean narrative that matches what they think you want to hear. The switch story approach forces them to recall the specific moment and context, which surfaces the real forces. Don't shortcut to the outcome; the journey is the data.
