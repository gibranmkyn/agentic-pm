# Research Questions: Power User Deep-Dive

## Goal
Map the specific workflows and habits your most engaged users have built around your product — so you can design for those patterns and find the features that are hiding in power users' workarounds.

## Screener criteria
- In the top 10–15% by usage frequency or feature depth (not just logins — define your engagement metric first)
- Has been active for at least 3 months
- Uses the product for work, not just occasional personal tasks
- Avoid users who are paying for your highest tier solely because their company gave them a license — you want people who actively chose to be heavy users

## Opening (5 min)

1. How long have you been using [product] and what first got you using it heavily?
2. Can you give me a rough picture of your week — when do you open [product], and for what?
3. Would you say [product] is something you depend on, or something you use when it's convenient?

*Purpose: establish usage intensity and context before going into specifics.*

## Core questions (40–45 min)

### The workflow
4. Walk me through the last time you used [product] for something important. What were you trying to accomplish?

   *Probe: Take me step by step — what did you do first, then what? Show me if it's easier.*

5. Is that a workflow you do regularly, or was it a one-off?

   *Probe: How often do you do this? Has the way you do it changed since you first started?*

6. Are there steps in your workflow that you've figured out yourself — things you've learned or discovered through trial and error that aren't obvious?

   *Probe: Where did you learn that trick? Did anyone else show you, or did you figure it out?*

### The evolution
7. How has your use of [product] changed since you first started? What do you use now that you didn't use at the beginning?

   *Probe: Was there a specific thing that changed how you thought about the product — a feature you discovered, a situation you were in?*

8. Is there a feature you now consider essential that you ignored for a long time?

   *Probe: What made you finally try it? What held you back before?*

### The gaps
9. Are there things you do around [product] — in other tools, spreadsheets, workarounds — that you wish you could do inside it?

   *Probe: Walk me through one of those workarounds. How often do you do this?*

10. Is there something [product] does that's almost right but not quite — something you use but feel slightly frustrated by?

    *Probe: What would "right" look like for you?*

### The stickiness
11. If [product] disappeared tomorrow, what would you do? Could you get by with something else?

    *Probe: What specifically would you lose that matters most?*

12. Have you ever come close to stopping — had a moment where you thought "this isn't worth it"?

    *Probe: What resolved that? Was it a product improvement, a change in your situation, or just getting used to the friction?*

### Spreading it
13. Have you ever recommended [product] to someone else or shown them how you use it?

    *Probe: What did you show them? What made you think it would be useful for them?*

14. When you've shown someone else how you use it, what's the thing that usually makes them go "oh, I didn't know you could do that"?

## Closing (5 min)

15. If you had 15 minutes with the team that builds [product], what's the one thing you'd tell them to fix or build?

16. Is there anything about how you use it that you think most users probably don't do — something that makes a big difference for you?

## What to code for
- **Evolved workflows:** How has their use changed from first use to now? The evolution story shows what "getting good at the product" looks like
- **Non-obvious features:** Features they discovered through trial and error — signals of discoverability or education gaps for less engaged users
- **Workarounds:** What are they doing outside the product that they want inside it? These are the next features
- **Stickiness reasons:** The specific things they'd lose if the product disappeared — these are the moats
- **Teaching moments:** What do they show other people? That's the product's most explainable value proposition

## Common mistake with this guide
Asking power users what features to build next and treating their answers as a roadmap. Power users are not representative of new or mid-tier users — their needs are advanced and their workarounds are sophisticated. The most valuable thing you learn from power users isn't their feature requests; it's the workflows and mental models that make them power users. Study those, then ask what a mid-tier user would need to develop those same patterns.
