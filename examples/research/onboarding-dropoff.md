# Research Questions: Onboarding Drop-off

## Goal
Identify where and why new users stop making progress in onboarding — so the team can distinguish between comprehension gaps, motivation gaps, and product gaps.

## Screener criteria
- Signed up within the last 3 weeks
- Did not reach the activation milestone (define specifically: first [action] — e.g., first project created, first task completed, first connection made)
- Still have an account — didn't explicitly delete or cancel
- Exclude users who signed up for a specific one-time use (e.g., downloaded a single file) — they were never trying to activate

## Opening (5 min)

1. Can you tell me what made you sign up for [product] in the first place?
2. What were you hoping to accomplish with it?
3. How has it been going since you signed up?

*Purpose: establish their original intent, which you'll use to evaluate whether onboarding served that intent.*

## Core questions (30–35 min)

### The sign-up context
4. Walk me through the day you signed up. What were you doing, and what made you decide to try it then?

   *Probe: Was this something you'd been meaning to do for a while, or did you sign up on impulse?*

5. When you first got in, what did you try to do?

   *Probe: Did you follow the onboarding steps, or did you try to do your actual task first?*

### The friction point
6. At some point, did you get stuck or not know what to do next?

   *Probe: What happened at that point — did you try to figure it out, ask for help, or just stop?*

7. Was there a moment where you thought "I don't understand what this is asking me to do"?

   *Probe: What was the specific step or screen?*

8. Was there a moment where you understood what to do but it felt like more work than expected?

   *Probe: What made it feel like too much?*

### Motivation and expectation
9. Did [product] do what you expected it to do in the first session?

   *Probe: What was different from what you expected?*

10. After your first session, how did you feel about whether this was worth continuing?

    *Probe: What would have made you more confident it was worth it?*

### The current state
11. Have you logged in since your first session?

    *If yes:* What brought you back? What did you try?
    *If no:* What would need to change for you to go back and try again?

12. Is [product] still something you're considering using, or have you moved on?

    *Probe: What would you need to see to change your mind?*

### What would good look like
13. If onboarding had gone perfectly — if you'd gotten exactly what you needed in the first session — what would you have been able to do at the end of it?

    *Probe: Was that possible with the product as it is, and you just didn't find it?*

## Closing (5 min)

14. If you were redesigning the first 10 minutes of [product] for someone exactly like you, what's the one thing you'd change?

15. Is there anything about your experience I haven't asked about that you think is important?

## What to code for
- **Comprehension gaps:** "I didn't know what to do" — steps or terminology that weren't clear
- **Motivation gaps:** "I understood but it felt pointless" — unclear value promise at that step
- **Effort gaps:** "It was too much work to get started" — friction that isn't comprehension-related
- **Expectation mismatches:** Signed up expecting X, got Y — often reveals acquisition messaging vs. product disconnect
- **Original intent:** What did they actually want to accomplish? Did onboarding serve that intent?

## Common mistake with this guide
Watching users complete a scripted onboarding task instead of asking them to do what they originally came to do. Lab usability with a scripted task tells you whether users can follow instructions. It doesn't tell you whether they were ever motivated to follow those instructions in the first place. The most common onboarding failure is not a UX failure — it's a motivation failure. The user understood the steps but didn't believe the steps would get them where they wanted to go.
