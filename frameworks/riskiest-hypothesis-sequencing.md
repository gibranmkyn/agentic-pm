# Riskiest Hypothesis Sequencing

The standard response to strategic uncertainty is "build an MVP." But MVPs default to validating the value proposition — and the value proposition may not be your riskiest assumption. Riskiest hypothesis sequencing is the discipline of finding the actual highest-risk unknown and attacking that first, regardless of which dimension it sits in.

The framework is tightly coupled with the [Six Dimensions of Product Strategy](six-dimensions-of-product-strategy.md): the six dimensions produce the hypotheses; this framework sequences the validation order.

**Author:** Sachin Rekhi — *Deliberate Startup* (Reforge, 2022). See [sachinrekhi.com](https://sachinrekhi.com).

---

## The core principle

Validation resources — time, customer access, development — are scarce. The highest return on these resources is eliminating the largest unknowns first. Validating what you already believe, or what is easiest to test, creates a false sense of progress while the real risk goes untested.

---

## How to identify the riskiest hypothesis

1. **Write out your six dimensions explicitly** — see [Six Dimensions of Product Strategy](six-dimensions-of-product-strategy.md). Don't skip any.
2. **For each dimension, ask:** How much conviction do we have? What evidence supports it? What would falsify it?
3. **Identify the dimension where conviction is lowest**, evidence is thinnest, or negative feedback has already appeared.
4. **That is your riskiest hypothesis.** Validate it first.

---

## Validation technique by hypothesis type

Different hypotheses require different validation techniques:

| Hypothesis type | Appropriate validation |
|---|---|
| Value proposition | Customer interviews, prototype testing, MVP |
| Target customer definition | Customer interviews, attribute clustering, segment analysis |
| Business model / pricing | Pre-sales, paid pilots, pricing experiments |
| Growth strategy / acquisition | Channel experiments, viral coefficient measurement, paid tests |
| Market size | Analog analysis, anti-log analysis, market research |
| Strategic differentiation | Competitive teardowns, win/loss analysis, customer switching interviews |

**The MVP trap:** a free MVP with manually-recruited customers validates value proposition but cannot validate pricing, acquisition cost, or viral growth. If your riskiest hypothesis is about any of those, the MVP is the wrong tool.

---

## The iterative loop

Riskiest hypothesis sequencing isn't one-time:

1. Identify the riskiest hypothesis
2. Pick the right validation technique for that hypothesis type
3. Run the validation
4. Update conviction across all six dimensions
5. Identify the *new* riskiest hypothesis → repeat

Each round should increase conviction on the highest-risk unknown. The goal is an efficient spiral toward overall conviction, not a linear march through every dimension.

---

## Common failure modes

| Failure | Fix |
|---|---|
| Defaulting to value proposition validation | Ask first: "Is value prop actually the riskiest unknown?" |
| Free MVP when business model is riskiest | If you don't know if people will pay, give the product away for free actively prevents you from finding out |
| Validating what you already believe | Force yourself to state conviction levels honestly; what would change your mind? |
| One round of validation and done | Validation is iterative; conviction must be updated after each round |

---

## When NOT to use it

- When you already have strong conviction on all six dimensions — the framework has served its purpose; move to execution.
- Post-PMF, when the product is in growth phase — focus shifts from hypothesis validation to optimization and scaling.
