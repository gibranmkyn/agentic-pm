# Research Questions: Retention Drivers

## Goal
Identify the specific product behaviors, workflows, and contextual factors that keep mid-tier users engaged long-term — so the team can design for those patterns and reduce quiet churn.

## Screener criteria
- Active user for 3–12 months (exclude <3 months — too early to understand retention; exclude >12 months — habits are too entrenched to identify formation)
- Moderate usage: not in your top 10% by engagement, not in your bottom 20% (those are different problems)
- Uses the product in a context that matches your core use case (not edge case usage)
- Exclude users whose retention is primarily explained by switching cost or a mandate from their employer

## Opening (5 min)

1. How long have you been using [product], and roughly how often do you use it?
2. Is it something you use out of habit, because you have to, or because you actively choose it?
3. Can you remember back to when you first started — what were you expecting from it?

*Purpose: establish tenure, volition, and initial expectations before exploring current state.*

## Core questions (30–35 min)

### What keeps them coming back
4. What's the main thing [product] does for you that keeps you coming back?

   *Probe: Is it a task it helps you complete, something it saves you, or a feeling it gives you?*

5. Can you walk me through the last time [product] was genuinely useful — the last time it saved you time or helped you do something you couldn't have done easily without it?

   *Probe: What specifically happened? What would you have done instead?*

6. Is there a routine or habit you've built around [product]? When in your day or week do you reliably use it?

   *Probe: Was that habit something you developed naturally, or did you have to work at it?*

### What could break it
7. Has your use of [product] ever declined for a stretch — a period when you were using it less? What was going on then?

   *Probe: What brought you back to using it regularly?*

8. Is there anything about [product] that you put up with — something that's annoying or suboptimal, but not enough to make you stop?

   *Probe: How long have you been living with that? Have you tried to work around it?*

9. If you were going to stop using [product], what would most likely cause it?

   *Probe: Is it a product thing, a life-context thing, or a competitive alternative thing?*

### The value core
10. What would you lose if [product] went away tomorrow?

    *Probe: Is that something you could replace? What would you replace it with?*

11. Is there something [product] does that you don't think most users notice or take advantage of — something that's a bigger deal for you than it might seem?

    *Probe: When did you discover that? Did it change how you used the product?*

### Relationship with the product
12. Has [product] gotten better or worse since you started using it?

    *Probe: What changed? Was it a specific feature, a performance improvement, or something about how it fits your life now vs. then?*

13. Do you feel like [product] knows how you use it, or does it feel like it was built for someone slightly different?

    *Probe: What gives you that impression?*

### Network and habits
14. Are there other people in your life or work who also use [product]?

    *Probe: Does that affect how you use it? Would you use it if they didn't?*

## Closing (5 min)

15. If you were advising the [product] team on what to focus on to keep people like you engaged for the long term, what's the one thing you'd say?

16. Is there anything about your relationship with [product] — the way you use it, how important it is, what it means to you — that I haven't asked about?

## What to code for
- **Core value moment:** The specific thing the product does that they'd miss most — name it precisely
- **Retention habit:** Is there a routine or trigger that reliably brings them back? Can it be designed for?
- **Tolerance threshold:** What are they putting up with? What would push that tolerance past the breaking point?
- **Churn trigger:** What would most likely cause them to leave? This is often a context change (life event, job change) not a product failure
- **Value clarity:** Can they articulate what makes the product worth staying for? If they can't, it's at risk even if they're currently engaged

## Common mistake with this guide
Running this with your most enthusiastic users because they're easier to recruit and more fun to talk to. Power users will give you rich stories about value delivered, but their retention drivers are not the retention drivers of your median user. The mid-tier user has different reasons to stay — often more fragile, more habitual, less articulated. They're the users who leave quietly, and they're the segment this guide is designed to reach.
