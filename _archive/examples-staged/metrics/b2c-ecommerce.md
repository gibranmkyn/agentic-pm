# Metrics: B2C E-commerce (DTC) — Monthly Repeat Purchase Rate

## North Star Metric
**Monthly Repeat Purchase Rate** — The first purchase is acquired; the second is earned. Repeat purchase rate is the single clearest signal that the product experience — not just the ad spend — is creating value. It predicts LTV far better than first-order AOV, and it's the only metric that distinguishes a real DTC brand from a one-time arbitrage play.

Core action: **Making a second purchase within 60 days** — The 60-day window captures whether the first purchase experience was good enough to bring someone back while the product is still top of mind. Customers who repurchase within 60 days are 4x more likely to become long-term loyalists (standard DTC industry observation).

## Metric tree

Monthly Repeat Purchase Rate
├── Product quality & satisfaction
│   ├── Post-purchase NPS (day 7 after delivery)
│   └── Return rate (% of orders returned for product reasons, not shipping)
├── Re-engagement
│   ├── Email open rate for post-purchase sequences
│   ├── Repurchase prompt click-through rate
│   └── Time-to-second-purchase (median, by acquisition cohort)
└── Loyalty depth
    ├── 3rd+ purchase rate (12-month cohort)
    ├── Subscription opt-in rate (if subscription tier exists)
    └── Referral rate among 3+ purchase customers

## Guardrail metrics
- Net promoter score — guards against optimizing for repurchase volume at the expense of product quality (e.g., aggressive email sequences that pull forward repurchases but burn trust)
- Margin per order — guards against discounting strategies that inflate repeat rate while destroying unit economics

## What to watch at each stage
- **Pre-PMF:** Are any customers repurchasing without a discount or re-engagement email? Organic repurchase behavior is the PMF signal. Discount-driven repurchase is not.
- **Growth:** Cohort LTV curves — are later acquisition cohorts (Q3, Q4) retaining as well as early cohorts, or is the repeat rate diluting as you move down the funnel?
- **Mature:** Referral rate among loyal customers (3+ purchases). At maturity, word-of-mouth from existing loyalists is the cheapest acquisition channel. If it's flat despite high repeat rates, the brand isn't generating advocacy — just habit.

## Common mistake for this product type
Measuring repeat purchase rate on all customers and calling it healthy. The number is easy to inflate with email discounts. Segment it: organic repeat rate (no discount, no triggered email within 7 days) vs. discount-assisted. If organic repeat rate is below 20% at growth stage, the product itself has a retention problem that email is masking — and the masking is expensive.
